Choosing between 433 MHz and 868 MHz may seem purely technical, but it's a strategic decision that influences equipment compatibility, actual coverage in your area, and the practical usefulness of your system.
Whether it's a personal project amongst friends or something more extensive, before buying or defining which frequency to use, it's essential to conduct a detailed assessment of your situation.

## Fundamental Questions to Ask

### 1. Do I already have equipment?

If you already own devices, check which frequency they operate on. Changing to a different frequency may mean:

- Leaving equipment unused
- Having to invest in new equipment

---

### 2. Who do I want to communicate with?

- Is it just friends in the same block or neighbourhood?
- Do you want to communicate with a more extensive network installed in various locations across Portugal?

If the intention is only local communication between a small group, the frequency may matter less. But if you intend to communicate with a larger group of users spread geographically, it makes sense to choose the frequency most used in the areas you want to communicate with.

---

### 3. What is the dominant frequency in your area?

Before deciding, visually check which frequency has the most coverage near you — this helps avoid buying equipment that ends up isolated because nobody around uses the same frequency.

You can analyse mesh network coverage maps to see which frequencies and networks are used in different locations:

🗺️ **MeshCore Portugal coverage map**  
➡️ https://www.meshcore.pt/en/mesh/map

This map shows repeaters and nodes registered in Portugal and can help you understand which frequency has the greatest presence in your region.

🗺️ **Global MeshCore map**  
➡️ https://meshcore.co.uk/map.html

This global map shows the presence of nodes, clients, and repeaters registered internationally, allowing you to identify frequency usage patterns in various areas.

Analysing these maps gives you a concrete view of the existing network reality — something far more valuable than simply reading technical specifications.

---

### 4. What is the range and operating environment?

Frequency also influences:

- Penetration through obstacles (walls, trees, buildings)
- Range capability in urban vs. rural areas
- Permitted transmission power

Although frequency alone isn't everything, in many modern networks the range also depends on the presence of adjacent repeaters and nodes — something the maps above can show.

---

### 5. Will I use an existing network or create my own?

Decide whether your intention is to:

- Create an exclusively local network
- Communicate with the Portugal network as shown on the maps

---

## Practical Cases

### Case 1 — Only want to communicate locally (e.g., a block)

If your intention is only to talk with friends in a block, the frequency may matter less. In these cases, you can choose frequency based on:

- Module price
- Immediate availability
- Ease of configuration

Interoperability with external networks is less relevant here.

---

### Case 2 — Communicate with the wider network in Portugal/EU

If you intend to communicate with the rest of the network that already exists across Portugal or in other countries, then it's advisable to analyse:

- What frequency is most used in that network?
- Where are the repeaters/are there already nodes on the 433 or 868 band?

Use the maps indicated above to see where nodes and repeaters exist in your area and which band (433 MHz or 868 MHz) is most present before buying equipment.

---

### Case 3 — Already have equipment on one band but want to expand

Imagine you already have 433 MHz modules but, according to the maps, the community around you (or the network you want to join) is growing mainly on 868 MHz. You could consider:

- Keeping 433 equipment for local use
- Buying new 868 equipment to connect to the existing network
- Using a gateway/bridge that interconnects both frequencies

---

## Conclusion: Analyse Reality on the Ground

Choosing between 433 MHz and 868 MHz involves more than simply looking at numbers or technical specifications. You must consider:

- The equipment you already have
- Who you want to communicate with
- What the predominant frequency is in your area
- Whether you intend to be part of a larger network
- The physical range you need

One of the best ways to make this decision is to consult coverage maps — like those mentioned above — to see where nodes and repeaters are already installed and which frequency is most active in your surroundings.
